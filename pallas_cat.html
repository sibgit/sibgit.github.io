Pallas's cat (Otocolobus manul)

Habitat and distribution

The Pallas's cat (Otocolobus manul) is distributed across a broad region of Central Asia, ranging from the Caucasus and the Iranian Plateau through Mongolia and southern Siberia to the Tibetan Plateau and parts of the Himalayas. It inhabits cold, dry environments such as rocky grasslands, montane steppes, shrublands, scree slopes, and high-altitude plateaus, typically in areas with limited snow cover. Well adapted to harsh continental climates, the species relies on rocky outcrops, crevices, and abandoned burrows for shelter and protection from predators and extreme weather.

Diet / behavior / social organization

The Pallas's cat is a solitary and elusive predator that spends most of its life alone, interacting with other individuals mainly during the breeding season. It shelters in rock crevices, caves, and abandoned burrows, and is typically most active at dawn and dusk. Its diet consists primarily of small mammals, especially pikas, voles, gerbils, ground squirrels, and other rodents, although it may also consume birds and insects. Rather than pursuing prey over long distances, the species relies on camouflage, stealth, and ambush hunting. Females raise their kittens alone in protected dens until they become independent.

What makes this animal awesome

With the densest fur of any wild cat and remarkable adaptations for camouflage and survival in harsh mountain steppes, the Pallas's cat is one of the most unique and specialized feline predators in the world. There's something both relaxing and intriguing about looking at a Pallas's cat's face.
